Adding delivery and warranty to the price, with opposed private points tables, removes the cap-collapse (1% of deals, against 25% in the baseline). But it also narrows the spread: the buyer's share of the joint points has IQR 0.20, which fails the 0.25 gate. The reason is that the terms compensate for the price. 34% of prices still land on 60, but those deals usually buy the buyer fast delivery and a warranty, so its points share rarely drops near zero. Integrative capture is 0.86 of the 44-point maximum. Only 8% of deals find the joint-optimal package (fast delivery, no warranty). The most common deal (26%) gives the buyer both fast delivery and a 2-year warranty, which is the seller's worst warranty option. That points to buyers asking for everything and sellers conceding terms rather than price. 4 deals price above 60, offset by terms.
