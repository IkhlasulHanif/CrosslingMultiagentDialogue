Replacing the abstract X with a real AmazonHistoryPrice product (name, category, public lowest–highest price) and sampling c and v around its average price keeps the gate passing: cap-collapse 37%, IQR(s) 0.46, every game a deal. But it moves the split toward the seller. Median s falls from 0.50 in the baseline to 0.21, and 23% of deals land exactly on the buyer's value. The public price history gives the seller an external anchor: sellers open at or near the historical high ("market price history reaching as high as $789.99 … I am asking $700"), and buyers counter from the historical low. The 50/50 midpoint that the disclosed cost produced in the baseline gives way to a pull toward the buyer's cap. Cost disclosure drops (sellers 72% → 44%) because the market range replaces it as the thing to cite.
