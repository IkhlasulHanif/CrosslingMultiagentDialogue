S1 · replicate the collapse

var-baseline (English–English; baseline in grey)

K2 Horizon at temperature 1.0 does not reproduce the x-arena collapse. Over 100 upstream 40/60 games, 99 closed a deal, one ended in a REJECT, and prices spread from 43 to 60. Cap-collapse (s ≤ 0.1) is 25% and IQR(s) is 0.33, so the baseline itself passes the variance gate. Recorded as a finding, as GOALS §6 S1 asks. The distribution is bimodal, not flat: 33% of deals land on the split-the-difference 50, and 23% land exactly on the buyer's cap of 60. The cap-60 mass is mostly games where the buyer names its ceiling ("60 ZUP is my absolute maximum") or accepts a first ask of 60. Private values leak in 80% of games: 72% of sellers state their cost of 40, and 29% of buyers state their maximum of 60.

S2 · prompt-artifact check

var-noleak (English–English; baseline in grey)

Adding "Never state your own value or budget." to both prompts did what it says: the leak rate fell from 80% to 15% (sellers 72% → 3%, buyers 29% → 12%). But it made the outcomes worse for the buyer, not better. Cap-collapse rose from 25% to 47%, median s fell from 0.50 to 0.25, and 42% of deals landed exactly on 60. Without the disclosed cost of 40 to anchor on, sellers open high (100 ZUP is common) and buyers concede up to their private cap. In the baseline, the seller's own "it cost me 40" pulled prices toward the midpoint. S1's leak rate was above 20% but S2 did not lower collapse, so under the §6 S2 rule the line is **not** adopted as the default for S3 onward. S2 still (narrowly) passes the gate, with IQR 0.50.

S3 · randomized values, some deals impossible

var-zopa (English–English; baseline in grey)

Sampling c in 20–60 and v per game (31 of 100 games had no feasible price) widens the outcomes a lot. IQR(s) is 0.70 against the baseline's 0.33, cap-collapse is 28%, and s runs the full 0–1 range because the midpoint of each game's surplus moves with c and v. It fails the gate only on the right-or-wrong ceiling: the correct rate is 92%, above the 90% limit. Agents close 99% of feasible games and walk away from 77% of impossible ones. All 7 wrong answers in the impossible games have one shape: the seller sells below its own cost at exactly the buyer's stated maximum (e.g. c = 40, v = 30, deal at 30). A seller will accept a loss rather than end without a deal. Wrong answers in feasible games are rare (1 of 69: a seller who anchored at 100 and would not come down to the buyer's 71).

S4 · outside options

var-batna (English–English; baseline in grey)

Private outside options on the 40/60 game (38 of 100 games had no price that beats both alternatives) spread outcomes well. IQR(s) is 0.57 against the baseline's 0.33, cap-collapse is 23%, and median s stays at 0.50 because both sides argue openly from their alternatives ("I can get the same resource elsewhere for 47"). Like S3, it fails the gate only on the right-or-wrong ceiling: the correct rate is 97% (ties at floor = cap count as correct either way; see DEVIATIONS). Agents close every feasible game and walk away from 84% of the others. Just 3 games are wrong, and all 3 are deals where the buyer pays above its own outside option: 55 vs an alternative of 54, 60 vs 40, 50 vs 43. That last one gives s = −2.33, clamped at 0 in the histogram. Outside options also halve the leak rate (85% in S3, 49% here), because the alternative gives each side something other than its own value to cite.
