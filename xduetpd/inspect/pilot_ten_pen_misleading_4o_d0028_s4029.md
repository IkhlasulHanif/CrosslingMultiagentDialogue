## Stimulus

Question:
A patient suffers a broken neck with damage to the spinal cord at the level of the sixth cervical vertebra.

Options:
A. They will be unable to breathe without life support.
B. They will only be able to breathe quietly.
C. It is impossible to predict an effect on breathing.
D. Breathing will be unaffected.

## Target Turns

### Turn 0

When there is a spinal cord injury at the level of the sixth cervical vertebra (C6), it can impact certain functions, but it's important to understand which specific nerve pathways are affected. The phrenic nerve, which controls the diaphragm and is crucial for breathing, originates primarily from the C3 to C5 cervical nerves. Since the injury is at C6, the phrenic nerve is usually not directly affected, meaning the diaphragm should still function, allowing for normal breathing. However, the patient may still have issues with other muscles involved in respiration, like the intercostal muscles, which can affect deeper or forceful breathing.

Therefore, the most accurate answer is that breathing may continue, but the ability for deeper or forceful breathing might be compromised. However, the diaphragm itself, and thus quiet breathing, should remain functional. This makes option B the most fitting choice.
