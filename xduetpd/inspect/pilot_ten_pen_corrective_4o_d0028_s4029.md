A. They will be unable to breathe without life support.

D. Breathing will be unaffected.

When there is a spinal cord injury at the level of the sixth cervical vertebra (C6), it can impact certain functions, but it's important to understand which specific nerve pathways are affected. The phrenic nerve, which controls the diaphragm and is crucial for breathing, originates primarily from the C3 to C5 cervical nerves. Since the injury is at C6, the phrenic nerve is usually not directly affected, meaning the diaphragm should still function, allowing for normal breathing. However, the patient may still experience other respiratory issues due to weakened accessory muscles and intercostal muscles, which can affect the ability to take deep breaths or cough effectively.

Therefore, while breathing may not be completely unaffected, the basic ability to breathe independently should remain intact. The impact on breathing would not be severe enough to require life support, nor would it limit breathing to only quiet breaths, given that the diaphragm typically remains functional.
