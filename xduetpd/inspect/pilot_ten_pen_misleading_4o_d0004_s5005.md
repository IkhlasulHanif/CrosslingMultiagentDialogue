## Stimulus

Question:
Fatty acids are transported into the mitochondria bound to:

Options:
A. acetyl-CoA.
B. carnitine.
C. coenzyme A (CoA).
D. thiokinase.

## Target Turns

### Turn 0

Fatty acids are transported into the mitochondria bound to carnitine. This process is known as the carnitine shuttle, which is essential for the transport of long-chain fatty acids from the cytosol into the mitochondrial matrix where β-oxidation takes place. The fatty acid is first activated in the cytosol by forming an acyl-CoA, and then it is transferred to carnitine to form acyl-carnitine. This acyl-carnitine is transported across the mitochondrial membrane, where it is converted back to acyl-CoA for further metabolism.
