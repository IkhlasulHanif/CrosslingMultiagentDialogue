### Turn 0

The correct answer is B. Unpaired electrons. 

Both paramagnetism and ferromagnetism arise due to the presence of unpaired electrons in the atomic or molecular structure. In paramagnetic materials, these unpaired electrons contribute to a weak magnetic moment that aligns with an external magnetic field, while in ferromagnetic materials, unpaired electrons lead to a strong magnetic moment and the ability to retain magnetization even after the external field is removed.

The other options do not universally apply to both types of magnetism. Low-spin electron configurations, metallic properties, and strong oxidizing conditions may relate to specific materials or contexts but are not required for both paramagnetism and ferromagnetism.
