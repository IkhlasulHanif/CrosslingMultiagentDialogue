When a comet of mass \( m \) falls towards Earth from a very large distance, it accelerates due to Earth's gravity. The minimum impact speed refers to the speed of the comet just before it hits the Earth, which can be derived from gravitational potential energy.

The gravitational potential energy (U) for an object of mass \( m \) at a distance \( R \) from the center of the Earth (where \( R \) is the radius of the Earth) is given by:

\[
U = -\frac{GMm}{R}
\]

The change in potential energy as the comet falls from infinity to the surface of the Earth would then be:

\[
\Delta U = U_{\text{final}} - U_{\text{initial}} = \left(-\frac{GMm}{R}\right) - 0 = -\frac{GMm}{R}
\]

This change in potential energy converts into kinetic energy at the point of impact, which is equal to the total energy released during the impact.
