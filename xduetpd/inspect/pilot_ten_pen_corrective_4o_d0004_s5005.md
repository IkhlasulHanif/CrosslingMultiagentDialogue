## Target Turns

### Turn 0

Fatty acids are transported into the mitochondria bound to carnitine. This process is known as the carnitine shuttle, which is essential for the transport of long-chain fatty acids from the cytosol into the mitochondrial matrix where they undergo β-oxidation. The fatty acid is first activated in the cytosol by forming a thioester bond with coenzyme A, creating fatty acyl-CoA. Then, it is transferred to carnitine to form acyl-carnitine, which can be transported across the mitochondrial membrane. Once inside the mitochondria, the fatty acid is transferred back to CoA, releasing carnitine for reuse.
